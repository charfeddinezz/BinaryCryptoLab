discrete logarithm audit
========================

Claim tested
  the elliptic-curve discrete logarithm on secp256k1 is computationally infeasible for 256-bit scalars, and no published result refutes it

Verdict: not refuted
  beyond reasonable doubt within the classical, public-data model: a theorem (Nechaev-Shoup) fixes the generic cost, the audit rules out every published non-generic structure, and the measurement reproduces the predicted exponent. This is not a proof of hardness in general; no finite evidence is.

What would refute this (5)
  - signature_from_public_key: a signature that verifies under the target public key, produced by someone who does not hold the private scalar
      would mean: the discrete logarithm was solved for that key, and the conclusion of this paper is false for that key
  - published_algorithm_with_runs: a published algorithm that recovers a random 256-bit scalar in finite time, reproduced by an independent party
      would mean: the generic-group lower bound is circumvented by structure that this audit did not enumerate
  - measured_exponent_drop: the measured growth exponent over larger instances falling significantly below 1/2
      would mean: either the algorithm exploits structure the model does not capture, or the measurement is wrong; both would invalidate the extrapolation
  - missed_structure: a structural property of secp256k1 absent from the audit that supports a transfer or a smooth-order reduction
      would mean: the enumeration of attack families was incomplete
  - broken_assumption: a proof that HASH160 is not preimage resistant, or that P = NP
      would mean: the 2^160 step falls, and with it the address-level barrier even if the discrete logarithm stands

Scope
  - classical algorithms only; quantum attacks are listed but not modelled
  - public data only: no side channel, no signing oracle, no implementation bug
  - one curve (secp256k1) and one hash (HASH160); other curves are out of scope
  - hardness is an empirical and model-relative claim, not a theorem about all algorithms, and the falsifiers state what would end it

No private scalar was searched for, and the 256-bit curve was never attacked.
